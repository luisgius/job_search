# Employer and opportunity coverage strategy

Independent critical product review, 9 September 2026. This review uses repository evidence and the recorded Poland trial; it did not run a live crawl, inspect the production tracker, or recheck job availability. Numerical gates below are proposed decision rules, not achieved results or statistically established targets.

**No, the project does not cover all companies or all their jobs. Its next objective should be more distinct, currently actionable jobs that this candidate would pursue, with an explanation of what was checked and what remains unknown.** Keep the Python pipeline and SQLite tracker. First expose losses and uncertainty, offer a bounded view of older open opportunities, and recover important known employers; expand discovery only when its marginal benefit beats those alternatives.

## What the evidence establishes

The [Poland trial](POLAND_TRIAL_2026-09-09.md) retrieved 18,630 regional rows from No Fluff Jobs, retained 624 data/AI junior-or-mid rows, and deduplicated those into 123 identities. After fixes, three listings survived the daily 72-hour policy and eight survived the exploratory replay. These numbers measure successive slices of one source, not companies covered or Polish market recall. Deduplication reduced repeated representations; its reduction is not automatically lost coverage.

The three fresh results were Ework, DCG and Scalo. Full-ad inspection found Polish requirements, with additional experience barriers at DCG and Scalo. Given the recorded roughly 1.5 years of experience and unconfirmed Polish proficiency, these are retrieval successes without established candidate suitability. The broader manual review found selective stretches at Lufthansa, Meniga and Allegro, plus a larger stretch at Amway. HERE's cached result returned 410 in that trial. These are dated observations, not current recommendations or proof that every manually found role was absent from every configured source.

Allegro's migration to SAP Harmony is a concrete retrieval gap; the existing adapter does not retrieve that new site. Just Join IT returned 503, which leaves coverage unknown. Neither failure can be repaired by merely extending freshness. No Kraków-office job survived the existing DS/ML title filter in the trial; that does not establish that Kraków had no relevant jobs.

The [earlier research](RESEARCH_2026-09-09.md) remains useful, but its delivery sequence is partly overtaken by the checkout. A [durable scoring backlog](SCORING_BACKLOG.md) and [synthetic fit evaluation harness](JOB_FIT_EVALUATION.md) now exist. The backlog implementation revalidates current observations, preserves admission bounds and respects application protections (`src/main.py:432–515`). Do not propose building either capability from scratch. Their presence also does not establish deployment, operational performance, or real candidate-fit accuracy.

## Separate the coverage questions

| Layer | Actual question and evidence | Product implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employer discovery | Do we know the employer and its current careers endpoint? `discover_company` probes slug candidates on eight supported board families (`src/sources/ats_boards.py:3109–3193`); it starts with a supplied name. | ATS resolution is not discovery of every employer. A correct board match also does not establish relevant vacancies. Preserve ambiguous, capped and unsupported results. |
| Retrieval | Did we successfully enumerate the intended job inventory? No Fluff Jobs filters categories/seniority inside its adapter (`src/sources/nofluffjobs.py:250–293`); Just Join IT applies request and local gates (`src/sources/justjoin_it.py:233–285`). | The central filter cannot recover a posting never emitted by an adapter. Measure adapter exclusions and pagination limits as well as pipeline totals. |
| Freshness | Was it published recently, first observed recently, or recently confirmed open? `is_fresh` primarily checks `posted_at`, with explicit undated behavior (`src/filters.py:562–596`). LinkedIn alerts assign email receipt time (`src/sources/linkedin_email.py:302–365`). | Three different clocks need different labels. A new alert is not proof of a new posting. An old posting may still deserve an application. |
| Titles and geography | Did restrictive rules suppress plausible roles before assessment? Title exclusion precedes inclusion (`src/filters.py:251–278`); `apply_filters` stops at the first failed stage (`src/filters.py:659–727`). | Funnel counts are order-dependent. Audit a rejected sample and counterfactual gates; do not interpret the first rejection as the only barrier. |
| Enrichment and fit | Is there enough evidence to judge mandatory language, experience, location and employment conditions? SmartRecruiters enriches the first capped slice of adapter results (`src/sources/ats_boards.py:1506–1562`). | Spend detail requests on plausible, uncertain jobs, then distinguish a supported match, a stretch, a blocker and insufficient evidence. |
| Processing and visibility | Did an eligible observed job reach useful human review? `score_jobs` still caps its batch (`src/scoring.py:497–581`); the surrounding durable backlog now retains admitted overflow. | Measure queue age, capacity rejection, expiry and unassessed jobs. A larger feed can increase delayed work rather than useful recommendations. |

“All companies covered” has no defensible denominator here. Report monitored employers, successfully checked employers, and supported endpoints separately. For jobs, report recall only against a dated, explicitly bounded manual benchmark, with missing or inaccessible inventories excluded from the recall denominator and counted as unknown. Never describe a supported ATS family as complete coverage of its customers.

Record the public careers frontend, application destination and underlying ATS as separate evidence fields. A branded frontend or vendor-linked application button does not prove the underlying platform, an available public API, or compatibility with an existing adapter. Preserve official URLs and observation dates, and leave unverified platform identity unknown.

## Candidate value and the review experience

Use a small digest with three visible views: **new alerts**, **older opportunities checked recently**, and **needs review**. Preserve the original date and provenance, show last availability check, and show why each item is present. The exploratory view should retain dismissal and application history and should not repeatedly relabel old inventory as new.

For this early-career data scientist, evaluate adjacent titles such as decision scientist, product analyst, experimentation analyst, quantitative analyst and graduate data roles. These are candidate families to test, not instructions to include all analysts or internships. Require relevant duties and skills; distinguish substantive statistical/ML work from unrelated reporting or support. Whether internships, relocation beyond Kraków, Warsaw hybrid attendance or particular contract arrangements are acceptable remains a preference question, not something inferred from years of experience.

Keep mandatory requirements, preferences and unknowns separate. Do not infer Polish proficiency, work authorization or a completed degree, and do not infer a language requirement from the advertisement's language. A two-year requirement may be a consciously chosen stretch for this candidate; a three-to-five-year minimum deserves a visibly stronger reservation. The candidate's pursue/dismiss judgment should label those distinctions instead of an opaque score deciding them silently.

The strongest criticism of “more coverage” is that it can manufacture a busier inbox while leaving the candidate with the same zero confirmed matches. The counter-criticism is equally important: perfect evaluation must not become a reason to delay a known useful lead. Keep a small manual discovery lane during measurement, and allow concrete high-value employer repairs without waiting for a complete benchmark.

## Phased delivery and acceptance gates

The phases describe increments, not a framework migration or a new agent team. One implementation owner can deliver them through the existing source, filter, digest and tracker contracts.

| Phase | Deliverable | Proposed acceptance gate and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 — Establish the baseline | Reconcile current backlog/eval work; validate on a supported Python environment; collect per-source and per-employer outcomes, gate counts, description completeness, queue state and review time. Freeze 50–100 labeled jobs, including rejected and unassessed examples. | Every pilot source run distinguishes success, empty, partial/capped, failed and not checked. Counts reconcile within each declared stage, with malformed/adapter-dropped rows accounted for where observable. Observe at least seven scheduled runs; verify backlog outage/overflow/expiry and application protections offline. Current research reports a Python 3.9.6 environment below the declared >=3.10 requirement; verify the current interpreter instead of assuming it changed. |
| 1 — Recover visibility | Keep 72-hour alerts; add a separate finite explorer, initially up to 90 days, with a 30-day default view and a capped undated review list. Reuse existing identity and history. | In a frozen replay, older/undated records never acquire new publication dates, duplicates do not become new opportunities, and old items remain separate from fresh alerts. Every top recommendation has a dated full-ad availability observation or an explicit unknown label. Over two weekly reviews, identify at least three additional pursue-worthy active jobs versus the same-source 72-hour baseline within 30 additional review minutes/week; otherwise narrow or defer the automated view. |
| 2 — Recover known employers and evidence | Start with 20 named target employers, including 10 Kraków-relevant employers, chosen independently of easy ATS support. Add official careers evidence and endpoint health to a small versioned data file or watchlist companion. Prioritize confirmed migrations and selective enrichment. | All 20 have a recorded resolution status, official identity evidence where found, and last check; unresolved is valid. For top-ten recommendations, achieve at least 90% full-requirement evidence coverage and show every remaining uncertainty. On a capped before/after replay, enrichment recovers at least as many human-labeled useful jobs with no greater request budget. A live pilot must separately validate that result. |
| 3 — Expand proven gaps | Test broader title families, then a bounded 20-employer discovery sample; expand the monitored universe toward 50, including 20 Kraków-relevant employers, only if justified. Compare manual research/existing alerts against one additional discovery channel. | On held-out examples, recover at least 80% of labeled relevant title rejects while adding no more than two unsuitable items per ten reviewed. For discovery, require at least five verified previously unmonitored employers and three net-new pursue-worthy active jobs over four weeks, with zero accepted false employer matches and <=60 maintenance/research minutes/week. Record sample sizes; if evidence is too sparse, call it inconclusive, never a pass. |
| 4 — Retain only productive integrations | Review source contribution, manual decisions and recruiting outcomes; add a new adapter only for repeated uncovered demand. | Keep a channel when its unique pursue-worthy yield per review hour beats or complements the manual baseline within the budget below. After four weeks without incremental useful jobs, move it to low-frequency/manual review rather than increasing crawl scope automatically. Reassess seasonal low yield before deleting useful employer evidence. |

These thresholds deliberately constrain a one-person workflow and need calibration with actual volume. Do not average away a severe eligibility error to meet a precision target. Promote title changes only after inspecting all changed decisions, and keep the explorer separate if its extra volume harms daily alert usefulness. Phase 2 known-employer repairs may start alongside Phase 1 when evidence is already concrete; the measurement work should not block a small, reversible repair.

## Measurement that can change a decision

The primary measure is **distinct active jobs newly found that the candidate would pursue per week**, paired with **distinct employers represented** and **candidate review minutes**. “Active” means explicit current posting/application evidence at the recorded check time; HTTP 200 alone is insufficient, and unknown availability is not a positive. Separate confirmed fit from accepted stretch and unresolved requirements.

Track marginal source contribution after deduplication: compare the union with and without the new source on the same observation period. Shared postings are still useful for corroborating freshness or requirements, but do not count as net-new jobs. Count unique employers separately from agencies/recruiters when the actual employer is unknown; do not manufacture employer identities from slug guesses.

For a fixed employer/date benchmark, calculate retrieved relevant active jobs divided by manually identified relevant active jobs, and publish both counts. Freeze near-duplicate groups and split labels by employer/time before tuning. Include hard-filter rejects, adapter rejects where available, snippets, undated jobs, required versus preferred language, multi-country restrictions and stale listings. Report source/region/title strata and failures alongside aggregate metrics so an easy-to-crawl sample cannot masquerade as market recall.

Measure precision among the first ten reviewed items when ten exist, recall on labeled rejects, and assessment coverage. An errored score is unassessed, not a negative. The current ten-case harness exercises contracts with scripted responses and only three binary synthetic labels; its documentation explicitly says it cannot establish model quality or precision@10. Extend the data and evaluate real judgments before changing a model. Interviews and recruiter responses are useful delayed outcomes, but application selection and employer behavior make them unsuitable as the sole ranking metric.

Use three evidence levels in release reports: deterministic fixture correctness, separately bounded live provider checks, and human usefulness. The Poland report's 2,256 passing offline tests establish only the first for that recorded checkout. This strategy review did not rerun them. A documentation-only change does not need another full application test run.

## Operating budget and failure policy

Treat the following as proposed pilot ceilings, not existing enforced settings or spending authorization:

- Reserve at most 60 minutes/week for employer/source maintenance and 30 extra minutes/week for exploratory job review. Measure these separately from normal applications. Begin with 20 employers because 50 at even two minutes each already exceeds the maintenance allowance before failures.
- Within that maintenance allowance, check endpoint health and changed plausible vacancies, refresh availability on the top ten recommendations, and use remaining time for failed or migrated endpoints. Do not commit to manually rereading every open job at every employer weekly; stable complete feeds can be reconciled automatically within their limits.
- Bound a discovery experiment to 20 employers, 100 search queries and 200 endpoint/detail HTTP attempts over four weeks, including retries and redirects. Check known official careers links before slug sweeps; eight board probes per name can consume much of this allowance. Record work skipped by the cap. These are real transport-attempt limits to implement and verify, not a claim that the current `RequestBudget` probe counter accounts for every transport request.
- Start selective enrichment at 20 extra detail attempts per normal run, with caching and per-host backoff; lower the cap if it delays the daily digest. Cache successful content with checked-at/provenance and keep transient errors distinct from verified closure. Shortlist availability evidence older than seven days needs refresh before being called recently checked.
- Use a proposed incremental provider-spend ceiling of €10/month only after current prices, currency conversion and enforcement are verified for the chosen provider. Until then compare requests, tokens and recorded cost with explicit unknown completeness. The evaluation report identifies incomplete production cost metering; recorded zero is not evidence of free operation. Prefer provider-side limits plus local attempt/token ceilings where supported.
- Alert on repeated employer-specific failures, exhausted pagination and a rising queue age; do not treat an empty result from an impaired source as no jobs. Report expiry and capacity refusal. A backlog admission is not a guarantee of later processing: a one-shot alert may never be observed again, and capacity rejection lacks the queue's durability guarantee.

Cheap gates before enrichment must use evidence they actually have. Apply definitive title/location exclusions, but defer uncertain or description-dependent geography, language, keyword and length judgments until after enrichment. Keep a small rejected-sample audit so optimization does not hide false negatives. On a provider outage, surface uncertainty and keep the bounded queue behavior; broad retries should not consume the entire day's budget.

## Alternatives and tradeoffs

| Tempting choice | Benefit | Strongest objection and better initial experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Increase the main freshness window to 90 days | Immediate larger inventory | Changes the meaning of daily alerts and repeats old jobs. Use a separate explorer with dates and history; measure incremental active useful jobs. |
| Add hundreds or thousands of guessed ATS slugs | Low effort to generate a long list | Name resolution cannot enumerate the market, may match the wrong company, and adds maintenance. Resolve a small externally chosen target list with official links. |
| Build an employer-registry service first | Central identity and migration history | A schema and UI can precede any demonstrated user gain. Begin with a versioned file and the existing watchlist; promote to a service only when actual updates and consumers require it. |
| Build a SAP Harmony adapter immediately | Addresses a demonstrated Allegro gap | One attractive stretch does not establish reusable adapter value. Keep a manual watch for that endpoint and implement when repeated useful vacancies or multiple priority employers justify fixtures and maintenance. A narrowly scoped repair can still win earlier if cheap. |
| Buy a search/extraction service | Finds employers outside supported ATS families | Search results can be stale, snippets omit requirements, and vendors may find the same jobs. Benchmark one channel against manual public careers research and existing alerts before a subscription or integration. No vendor accuracy winner is established by this review. |
| Remove title filters or use an LLM on every row | Potential recall increase | Pays to score irrelevant rows and can starve useful work. Test adjacent title families and a rejected sample under a fixed review/scoring budget. |
| Enrich every posting | More complete requirements | Duplicates and clearly unsuitable jobs consume requests. Select uncertain plausible jobs after deduplication, preserving an explicit insufficient-evidence state on failure. |
| Weekly reconciliation of “all open jobs” | Recovers older opportunities and closures | Only justified for complete authoritative inventories within known caps. Partial feeds and alerts do not establish closure through absence; annotate coverage and manually review unknowns. |
| New agent framework, autonomous crawler team or vector database | More general orchestration/search machinery | No demonstrated bottleneck requires them. Existing Python functions, fixtures and durable state support the proposed experiments with less operational work. |

## Peer review and scope

An early recommendation and criticism were sent to the coordinator before drafting. The coordinator proposed 50 named Poland employers, 20 relevant to Kraków, a careers/ATS registry and weekly open-job reconciliation. This review agrees with an explicit pilot denominator but challenges starting at 50, treating the registry as a service, and promising complete reconciliation of partial sources. It proposes 20 first, a lightweight evidence file, and expansion gated by time and unique useful yield. A fixed target list measures only that list, so keep an independent small discovery sample to avoid hiding employers outside it.

The market review challenged delaying a bounded Allegro collector behind measurement. Accepted: a small collector for the confirmed gap can proceed alongside observability if the official evidence and implementation cost justify it. That exception does not establish the value of a platform-wide SAP integration. Newly reported market observations were not independently checked in this review and are not counted as measured coverage gains here.
